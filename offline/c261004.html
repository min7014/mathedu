📋 [Problem Analysis & Core Concepts]

This problem tests the condition for a piecewise polynomial function to be continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ by matching one-sided limits at the boundary $x = a$.

📐 [Step-by-Step Rigorous Derivation]

Step 1: Equate left-hand and right-hand limits at $x = a$
$$\lim_{x \to a^-} (x^2 - 3x) = f(a) \implies a^2 - 3a = a - 4$$
Step 2: Solve the quadratic equation
$$a^2 - 4a + 4 = 0 \implies (a - 2)^2 = 0 \implies a = 2$$

Connecting each elementary-grounded scaffolding step from Step 1 to Step 5 completes the entire derivation without any logical gaps:

  • 🔰 Step 1 · [Elementary Arithmetic Link] Comparing two expressions at $x = 2$
      • Core Principle: When $x = 2$, $x - 4 = 2 - 4 = -2$ and $x^2 - 3x = 4 - 6 = -2$. Both expressions yield the exact same value $-2$!
      • Step Verification ($-2$): $2 \times 2 - 3 \times 2 = 4 - 6 = -2$.
  • 🌿 Step 2 · [Symbol Translation] Continuity means the two pieces meet at $x = a$
      • Core Principle: For the piecewise graph to be continuous without a break at $x = a$, substituting $x = a$ into both formulas must give the same value.
      • Step Verification ($a^2 - 3a = a - 4$): The left-hand limit $a^2 - 3a$ must equal the right-hand value $a - 4$.
  • 🌱 Step 3 · [Rearranging Terms] Moving all terms to the left side
      • Core Principle: Subtracting $a$ and adding $4$ to both sides of $a^2 - 3a = a - 4$ gives $a^2 - 4a + 4 = 0$.
      • Step Verification ($a^2 - 4a + 4 = 0$): Combining $-3a - a = -4a$ and moving $-4$ gives $a^2 - 4a + 4 = 0$.
  • 🌳 Step 4 · [Perfect Square] Factoring $a^2 - 4a + 4 = 0$
      • Core Principle: Two numbers that multiply to $+4$ and add to $-4$ are $-2$ and $-2$, so $a^2 - 4a + 4 = (a - 2)^2$.
      • Step Verification ($(a - 2)^2 = 0$): $a^2 - 4a + 4 = (a - 2)^2 = 0$.
  • 💎 Step 5 · [Final Answer] Solving $(a - 2)^2 = 0$ for $a$
      • Core Principle: Since the square of a number is $0$ only when the number itself is $0$, $a - 2 = 0 \implies a = 2$.
