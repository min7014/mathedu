The perpendicular bisector of chord passes through the center of the circle.

16-step visual proof · Geometry · Euclidean Plane Geometry

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 3

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions given in the problem on the diagram. Here, the starting conditions given by the perpendicular bisector passing through the chord AB of the circle and its midpoint M (AM = BM) and perpendicular to the chord AB are plotted with blue symbols.

  2. Step 4

    This is the most important eureka moment in this proof! Look at the new green line segment drawn on the shape. It is a line segment connecting A and B at the point on the two radii OA and OB (OA = OB = radius R) auxiliary line and perpendicular bisector that directly connect the center O of the isolated circle and the end points A and B of the chord. A relationship that was not visible through the original shape alone is divided into two comparable shapes by this single green auxiliary line!

  3. Steps 6–9

    The two radii OA and OB (OA = OB = radius R) that directly connect the center O of the isolated circle and the end points A and B of the chord (OA = OB = radius R) center on the line segment connecting A and B at the point on the auxiliary line and perpendicular bisector. Compare sides of the same length and angles of the same size one by one in the figure.

  4. Steps 10–11

    All points on the perpendicular bisector of the line segment are the same distance from both end points A and B (triangles PAM and PBM are congruent in SAS), and the center O of the circle also has the same length of the two radii OA and OB, so the principle that the center O must lie on the perpendicular bisector of the current AB is completed. What you must remember at this time is that the inevitable bridge that connects the center O of the circle in the air and the chord AB is the very definition of a circle: 'The radii drawn from the center to points A and B on the circle are the same (isosceles triangle)'!

  5. Step 12

    Compare the two areas highlighted in orange and purple. All points on the perpendicular bisector of a line segment are the same distance from both end points A and B (triangles PAM and PBM are congruent in SAS), and since the center O of the circle also has the same length of the two radii OA and OB, you can directly confirm the principle that the center O must lie on the perpendicular bisector of the current AB. Since the conditions for corresponding sides and angles are exactly met, the two shapes are perfectly congruent (or similar)!

  6. Step 16

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting conditions given by the chord AB of the circle, which was the starting condition, and the perpendicular bisector perpendicular to chord AB, passing through the midpoint M (AM = BM), the two radii OA, OB (OA = OB = radius R) directly connecting the center O of the isolated circle and both end points A and B of the chord, passing through the line segment connecting A and B at the point on the auxiliary line and the perpendicular bisector, the perpendicular bisector of the chord in the circle must be at the center O of the circle. It was clearly confirmed that the conclusion holds that the perpendicular line drawn from the center of the circle to the chord bisects the chord. The key point to remember here is that the inevitable bridge that connects the center O of the circle in the air and the string AB is the very definition of a circle: 'The radii drawn from the center to points A and B on the circle are the same (isosceles triangle)'! Finally, ask a deeper question. If the string AB is moved parallel to the outside of the circle and the two end points A and B merge into one point T to become a tangent line, how will the perpendicular bisector of the string naturally lead to the 'perpendicular line of the tangent line passing through the point of contact'?
