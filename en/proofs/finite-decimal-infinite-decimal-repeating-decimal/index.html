Finite Decimal, Infinite Decimal, Repeating Decimal

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Steps 2–10

    First, accurately establish the basic definition of the expression based on the starting condition of directly dividing the numerator by the denominator in a fraction (rational number) and observing whether the number after the decimal point ends or repeats endlessly. Before mechanically manipulating symbols, the first step is to recall the original meaning of each symbol.

  2. Steps 11–18

    Infinite decimal numbers shown on screen: Look at the relationship where there are infinitely many non-zero numbers below the decimal point. Now, we apply the number theory idea of decomposing the denominator of an irreducible fraction, which is the key to the proof, into prime factors and classifying whether the prime factors are only 2 or 5 (can be converted to a fraction raised to the power of 10), or whether there are prime factors other than 2 and 5. After this conversion, the structure of an expression that seemed complex can be clearly solved using basic operations that we already know.

  3. Steps 19–25

    Look at the loops: relationships presented on the screen. In division, the remainder is a natural number smaller than the dividing number (denominator), so if you divide by the maximum number of denominators, the remainder will become 0 (finite decimal) or the previously appearing remainder will always appear again, creating a circular node. According to the pigeonhole principle, the formula is derived without any gaps. What to pay special attention to at this stage is that not all infinite decimal numbers are irrational numbers! Repeating decimals (e.g. 0.333... = 1/3) are legitimate rational numbers that can be expressed as fractions, while only non-repeating interminating decimals (pi, square root of 2) are irrational.

  4. Steps 26–32

    Recursive decimal expression shown on screen: Look at the relationship above the numbers at both ends of the recursive node. If we combine the formulas organized in the previous step into one, we can finally naturally conclude that if we express the rational number we were aiming for as a decimal, it becomes a finite decimal with a finite number of non-zero numbers after the decimal point, or a cyclic decimal in which a certain number array (cyclic node) repeats infinitely!

  5. Step 33

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting condition of directly dividing the numerator by the denominator in a fraction (rational number) to observe whether the numbers after the decimal point end or repeat endlessly, we go through the number theory idea of classifying whether the denominator of the irreducible fraction has only 2 or 5 (can be converted to a fraction raised to the power of 10), or whether there are prime factors other than 2 and 5. When a rational number is expressed as a decimal, the number of non-zero numbers below the decimal point is finite. It was clearly confirmed that the conclusion holds that a finite decimal number or a certain number sequence (cyclic node) becomes a repeating decimal number. The key point to remember here is that not all infinite decimal numbers are irrational numbers! Repeating decimals (e.g. 0.333... = 1/3) are legitimate rational numbers that can be expressed as fractions, while only non-repeating interminating decimals (pi, square root of 2) are irrational. Finally, ask a deeper question. Multiply the repeating decimal x = 0.121212... by 100 to make 100x - x = 99x. How can we convert all repeating decimals to fractions in 1 second?
