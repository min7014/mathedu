(√(75)+(1/√(12)))÷ √(3)

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Steps 2–4

    First, for a positive number b, establish the basic definition of the division formula (a divided by b), which divides a number a by b, based on the given starting conditions. Before mechanically manipulating symbols, the first step is to recall the original meaning of each symbol.

  2. Steps 5–6

    Now, we apply the visual idea of dividing the unit bar or rectangular area model, which is the key to the proof, into b equal parts, and corresponding that 'one quotient divided by b' corresponds to 'the size multiplied by 1 times b'. After this conversion, the structure of an expression that seemed complex can be clearly solved using basic operations that we already know.

  3. Steps 7–9

    The quotient of division a divided by b is the number that becomes a by multiplying b, so if both sides are multiplied by 1 over b, which is the reciprocal of b, the equation is derived without any gaps according to the principle of the inverse of multiplication, which determines the quotient as exactly a times 1 over b. One thing to be especially careful about in this step is that when changing division to multiplication, be careful not to make the mistake of flipping the previous divisor number a! Only the last divisor, b, must be changed to its reciprocal (1 over b).

  4. Steps 10–11

    If we combine the formulas organized in the previous step into one, we can finally naturally come to the conclusion about the relationship between division and reciprocal that our goal, a divided by b, is always equal to 1 over b, which is the reciprocal of b, the dividing number (a times 1 over b)!

  5. Step 12

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting condition given the division formula (a divided by b) for dividing a certain number a by b for a positive number b, which was the starting condition, we divide the unit bar or rectangular area model into b equal parts and go through the visual idea that 'one quotient divided by b' corresponds to 'the size multiplied by 1 times b', and a divided by b is always the product of 1 over b, which is the reciprocal of the dividing number b (a times 1 over b). It was clearly confirmed that the conclusion of the relationship between division and reciprocal equals holds. The key point to remember here is that when changing division to multiplication, be careful not to make the mistake of flipping the preceding divisor number a! Only the last divisor, b, must be changed to its reciprocal (1 over b). Finally, ask a deeper question. If the dividing number b is not a natural number but a fraction (e.g. d over c), why can't we just multiply the denominator and numerator by the reciprocal (c over d)?
