Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Steps 5–7

    Now, we apply the key key to the proof, the geometric idea of aligning the vertices of a square with area a and a square with area b and overlapping them to visually compare the lengths (root a, root b) and the area of one side at the same time. After this conversion, the structure of an expression that seemed complex can be clearly solved using basic operations that we already know.

  2. Steps 8–10

    The equation is derived flawlessly according to the geometric monotonically increasing property that the larger the area of the square is, the longer the length of one side is, and the algebraic equivalence principle of squaring both sides and comparing them by removing the radical. One thing to be especially careful about at this stage is that when comparing natural numbers outside the radical and numbers inside the radical (e.g. 3 and the root of 10), 3 = root of 9 must be put inside the radical to ensure an accurate comparison.

  3. Steps 11–13

    If we combine the formulas organized in the previous step into one, we can finally naturally conclude that if the positive number a we were targeting is greater than b, the root a, which is the length of one side, is also greater than the root b, and conversely, if the root a is greater than the root b, the squared area a is also greater than b!

  4. Step 14

    The visual proof is complete!
