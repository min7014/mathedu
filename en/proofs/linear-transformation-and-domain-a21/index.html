Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 2

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions given in the problem on the diagram. Here, the starting conditions for applying a linear transformation that changes a21, the y component of the first-column basis vector e1(1,0) of the matrix to the unit square area of the coordinate plane, are drawn with blue symbols.

  2. Steps 3–4

    The starting conditions, which apply a linear transformation that changes a21, the y component of the first-column basis vector e1(1,0) of the matrix, to the unit square area of the coordinate plane, which is the starting condition, are drawn sequentially on the proof figure and the positional relationship between each point and line segment is clearly established.

  3. Steps 5–7

    Instead of manually substituting countless points inside the area, sides of the same length and angles of the same size in the shape are paired and compared one by one, centering on the base arrow auxiliary line that tracks which coordinates the two basis vectors, the horizontal reference arrow e1=(1,0) and the vertical reference arrow e2=(0,1), move to.

  4. Step 8

    Since the linear transformation always preserves the parallelism of straight lines and the equally spaced grid, the first and second column vectors of the matrix, which are the arrival points of the two basis vectors, soon become the two neighboring sides of the transformed parallelogram, completing the principle of performing a vertical push transformation that pushes the right side of the figure diagonally in the vertical direction while maintaining the horizontal width. What you must remember at this time is that it is easy to get lost if you try to solve a linear transformation problem only by substituting complex simultaneous equations! Always observe first as a geometric basis shift that (1,0) moves to the first column and (0,1) moves to the second column.

  5. Step 9

    Look at the final mark highlighted in red on the shape! Since the size of the corresponding part becomes the same due to the congruence and symmetry properties of the shape proven in the previous step, the conclusion has been finally proven that the geometric transformation law is established by which the unit square lattice performs a vertical push transformation in which the right side of the shape is pushed up diagonally in the vertical direction while maintaining the horizontal width, depending on the value of a21, the y component of the matrix element 1-column basis vector e1(1,0) that we targeted!

  6. Steps 11–13

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence relationship between the shapes, we can inevitably confirm that the geometric transformation law is established in which the unit square lattice performs a vertical shearing transformation in which the right side of the shape is pushed up diagonally in the vertical direction while maintaining the horizontal width, depending on the value of a21, the y component of the matrix component one-column basis vector e1(1,0) that we wanted to prove.

  7. Step 14

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting condition of applying a linear transformation that changes a21, the y component of the 1-column basis vector e1(1,0) of the matrix, to the unit square area of the coordinate plane, which was the starting condition, instead of inserting countless points inside the area one by one, the horizontal reference arrow e1=(1,0) and the vertical reference arrow e2=(0,1) go through the base arrow auxiliary line that tracks which coordinates the two basis vectors move to, and then the matrix components. It was clearly confirmed that, according to the value of a21, the y component of the first-column basis vector e1(1,0), a geometric transformation law is established in which the unit square grid performs a vertical push transformation in which the right side of the figure is pushed up diagonally in the vertical direction while maintaining the horizontal width. The key point to remember here is that if you try to solve a linear transformation problem only by substituting complex simultaneous equations, it is easy to get lost! Always observe first as a geometric basis shift that (1,0) moves to the first column and (0,1) moves to the second column. Finally, ask a deeper question.
