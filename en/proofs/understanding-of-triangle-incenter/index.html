Understanding of Triangle Incenter

11-step visual proof · Geometry · Euclidean Plane Geometry

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 3

    At I, the starting condition of the intersection of the bisectors of the two interior angles of a triangle, the assumption that the perpendiculars are placed on each of the three sides is drawn in turn on the proof figure, clearly establishing the positional relationship between each point and line segment.

  2. Steps 4–6

    Center on the three green perpendicular lines (inscribed circle radii) drawn perpendicular to the three sides of the triangle at intersection I and the auxiliary line connecting the third vertex, and compare sides of the same length and angles of the same size one by one.

  3. Step 7

    The right triangles divided by the bisector of the two internal angles become RHA congruent with the common hypotenuse + right angle + acute angle congruent, making the distances to all three sides the same, and the two right triangles at the last vertex become RHS congruent, completing the principle of bisecting the third angle. What you must remember at this time is that the circumcenter has the same distance to the 'three vertices' (OA=OB=OC), and the incenter has the same distance to the 'feet of the perpendicular lines on the three sides' (ID=IE=IF=r). It is also directly connected to the formula for the area of the triangle: S = 1/2·r·(a+b+c).

  4. Step 8

    Check the target shape to the right of the top arrow. This means that given the starting assumption on the left, the bisectors of the three interior angles to the right of the arrow meet at one point (incenter I), and the vertical distance from the incenter to all three sides is the same (radius r of the inscribed circle). This means that we will show why the conclusion is always true. Never mix assumptions and conclusions, and now let's build a logical bridge in the proof diagram below!

  5. Step 9

    This is the most important eureka moment in this proof! Look at the new green line segment drawn on the shape. It is an auxiliary line connecting the three green perpendicular lines (radius of the inscribed circle) drawn perpendicular to the three sides of the triangle at the intersection point I and the third vertex. A relationship that was not visible through the original shape alone is divided into two comparable shapes by this single green auxiliary line!

  6. Step 10

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence between shapes, we can confirm that the conclusion that the bisectors of the three interior angles we wanted to prove meet at one point (incenter I), and that the vertical distances from the incenter to all three sides are the same (radius r of the inscribed circle) is inevitably established.

  7. Step 11

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the assumption that the perpendicular lines are drawn on each of the three sides at the intersection point I of the bisectors of the two interior angles of the triangle, which was the starting condition, through the three green perpendicular lines (radius of the inscribed circle) drawn perpendicular to the three sides of the triangle at intersection I and the auxiliary line connecting the third vertex, the conclusion is that the bisectors of the three interior angles meet at one point (incenter I), and that the vertical distances from the incenter to the three sides are all the same (radius of the inscribed circle). It has been clearly confirmed that this is true. The key point to remember here is that the circumcenter has the same distance to the 'three vertices' (OA=OB=OC), and the incenter has the same distance to the 'foot of the perpendicular line on the three sides' (ID=IE=IF=r). It is also directly connected to the formula for the area of the triangle: S = 1/2·r·(a+b+c). Finally, ask a deeper question. If you draw the bisector of one interior angle of a triangle and the bisector of two exterior angles instead of the interior angles, what kind of circle (excircle) will be created outside the triangle?
