Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 1

    Today we explore Rationalization of Denominator (square root of a-square root of b, square root of a+square root of b) through a step-by-step visual proof. Before memorizing the final result, let us examine our starting condition and see why it inevitably leads to the conclusion.

  2. Steps 2–4

    First, accurately establish the basic definition of the expression based on the starting conditions given by the irrational fraction expression that contains a radical (root a, but a is a positive number) in the denominator. Before mechanically manipulating symbols, the first step is to recall the original meaning of each symbol.

  3. Steps 5–6

    Now, we apply the key to the proof, the algebraic idea of multiplying the denominator and numerator simultaneously by 1 (i.e. root a over root a), which does not change the size of the fraction, to make the denominator the square of (root a) = a. After this conversion, the structure of an expression that seemed complex can be clearly solved using basic operations that we already know.

  4. Steps 7–9

    Look at the b(root a −) relationship shown on the screen. The formula is derived flawlessly according to the principle of combining the basic property of fractions, which states that the value of the fraction is constant even if the denominator and numerator are multiplied by the same non-zero number, and the definition of the square root, which states that for a positive number a, the root a times the root a is the rational number a. Be especially careful in this step, be careful not to multiply only the denominator by root a and forget to multiply the numerator, or mistake root a times root a for 2 root a!

  5. Steps 10–11

    Look at the root a plus minus relationship shown on the screen. If we combine the formulas organized in the previous step into one, we can finally naturally come to the conclusion of rationalization of the denominator that if we multiply the root a of the denominator and numerator we were aiming for, the denominator will change to a rational number without a radical, resulting in the root a of b over a!

  6. Step 12

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting conditions given by an irrational fraction formula containing a radical (root a, but a is a positive number) in the denominator, the denominator and numerator are simultaneously multiplied by 1 (i.e. the root a of root a), which does not change the size of the fraction, and the algebraic idea of making the denominator become the square of (root a) = a. If the denominator and numerator are equally multiplied by the root a, the denominator is changed to a rational number without a radical, resulting in b root a of a. It was clearly confirmed that the conclusion of rationalization of the denominator holds. The key point to remember here is that you must be careful not to multiply only the denominator by root a and forget to multiply the numerator, or mistake root a times root a for 2 root a!
