The length of the space diagonal of 6-3-5 regular hexahedron

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 2

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions given in the problem on the diagram. Here, the geometric starting conditions for constructing a square with the three sides a, b, and c (hypotenuse c) of a right triangle and each side as one side are depicted with blue symbols.

  2. Steps 5–7

    Center on the auxiliary line that divides a large square with one side (a+b) into four congruent right triangles and a square in the middle (area c squared). Compare sides of the same length and angles of the same size one by one in the shape.

  3. Step 8

    Since the four corners of the right triangle are SAS congruent and the sum of the two acute angles is 90 degrees, one interior angle of the center square is 180 degrees minus 90 degrees = 90 degrees, making it a square. If you organize the equation for the total area, the principle of a squared plus b squared = c squared is completed. What you must remember at this time is that the center square can be a rhombus just because the four sides have the same length c, so it is perfect to prove that the interior angle is 90 degrees (square) by using the sum of the two acute angles to be 90 degrees!

  4. Step 9

    Look at the final mark highlighted in red on the shape! Since the size of the corresponding parts becomes the same due to the congruence and symmetry properties of the shapes proven in the previous step, the Pythagorean theorem and area conservation conclusion that the sum of the squares of the two sides with right angles, which we were aiming for, is always equal to the square of the hypotenuse (square of a plus square of b = square of c) have finally been proven!

  5. Steps 11–13

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence between shapes, we can confirm that the Pythagorean theorem and the area conservation conclusion that the sum of the squares of the two sides with right angles that we wanted to prove are always equal to the square of the hypotenuse (a squared plus b squared = c squared) are inevitably true.

  6. Step 14

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the geometric starting conditions in which a square is drawn with the three sides a, b, c (hypotenuse c) of a right triangle as one side and each side as the starting condition, the sum of the squares of the two sides containing the right angle is always equal to the square of the hypotenuse (the square of a (plus b squared = c squared) clearly confirmed that the Pythagorean theorem and the area conservation conclusion hold. The key point to remember here is that the center square can be a rhombus just because the four sides have the same length c, so it is perfect to prove that the interior angle is 90 degrees (a square) by using the sum of the two acute angles to be 90 degrees! Finally, ask a deeper question. If we place an equilateral triangle or a semicircle instead of a square on the three sides of a right triangle, will the sum of the areas of the two smaller shapes still be the same as the area of the hypotenuse shape?
