Trigonometric Ratio of 45 degree

14-step visual proof · Exponentials, Logs, Trig & Sequences · Trigonometry

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Proof at a glance

Starting condition
An isosceles right triangle ABC with ∠C = 90° and equal legs AC = BC = a (obtained by drawing one diagonal of a square)
What we prove
Acute angles ∠A = ∠B = 45° and exact side ratio 1 : 1 : √2 (so sin 45° = cos 45° = 1/√2 = √2/2, tan 45° = 1)
Key idea
Construct a square of side length a and draw its diagonal AB to partition it into two congruent isosceles right triangles
Proof logic
Since the two legs are equal (a = a), the base angles are 90° ÷ 2 = 45°; by the Pythagorean Theorem, AB = √(a² + a²) = √2·a, establishing the invariant ratio 1 : 1 : √2
Common misconception
Never confuse the hypotenuse ratio √2 with 2—always verify that (1)² + (1)² = (√2)²
What if not?
What side ratio emerges when we bisect an equilateral triangle instead of a square?

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 2

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions, which are the starting conditions given in the problem, on the diagram. Here, the starting conditions given by a right isosceles triangle (or a triangle bisected by a square diagonally) where the two sides of a right angle (90 degrees) are equal in length a and the two acute angles are each 45 degrees are drawn with blue symbols.

  2. Steps 3–4

    Proof that the starting conditions are a right-angled isosceles triangle (or a triangle bisected by a square diagonally) in which the two sides of a right angle (90 degrees) have the same length a and each of the two acute angles is 45 degrees, draw the starting conditions one by one on the figure and clearly establish the positional relationship between each point and line segment.

  3. Steps 5–7

    Based on the idea of dividing a square with side length a into two congruent right-angled isosceles triangles with sides a and a and base angles of 45 degrees by drawing a diagonal auxiliary line, compare sides of the same length and angles of the same size one by one.

  4. Steps 8–9

    Since the two base angles of an isosceles triangle are the same (180 degrees - 90 degrees) divided by 2 = 45 degrees, this is the stage where the principle that the length of the hypotenuse is determined by the root of 2 a is completed according to the Pythagorean theorem (a squared plus a squared = 2a squared). What you must remember at this time is that you should not mistake the length of the hypotenuse as 2a, which is the sum of the two sides! Since the sum of the squares is 2a squared, the positive square root of 2 a is the exact length of the hypotenuse.

  5. Step 10

    Look at the final mark highlighted in red on the shape! Since the sizes of the corresponding parts are the same due to the congruence and symmetry properties of the shapes proven in the previous step, the ratio of the lengths of the three sides of the right isosceles triangle that we aimed for is always 1 to 1 to the root of 2, and the conclusion that sin 45 degrees = 1/2 the root, cos 45 degrees = 1/2 the root, and tan 45 degrees = 1 is finally proven!

  6. Step 11

    Check the target shape to the right of the top arrow. Given the starting assumption on the left, the ratio of the lengths of the three sides of the right isosceles triangle to the right of the arrow is always 1 to 1 to the root of 2, which means that we will show why the conclusion that sin 45 degrees = root 1/2, cos 45 degrees = root 1/2, and tan 45 degrees = 1 always holds. Never mixing assumptions and conclusions, let's now build a logical bridge in the proof diagram below!

  7. Steps 12–13

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence between shapes, we can confirm that the ratio of the lengths of the three sides of the right isosceles triangle that we wanted to prove is always 1 to 1 to the root of 2, and that sin 45 degrees = 1/2 the root, cos 45 degrees = 1/2 the root, and tan 45 degrees = 1.

  8. Step 14

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting condition, a right-angled isosceles triangle (or a triangle bisected by a square diagonally) with two sides of right angles (90 degrees) having the same length of a and two acute angles of 45 degrees each, draw a diagonal auxiliary line on the square with one side of length a, dividing it into two congruent right-angled isosceles triangles with two sides a and a and two base angles of 45 degrees. After thinking through the idea, we clearly confirmed that the ratio of the lengths of the three sides of a right isosceles triangle is always 1 to 1 to the root of 2, and that sin 45 degrees = 1/2 the root, cos 45 degrees = 1/2 the root, and tan 45 degrees = 1. The key point to remember here is that you should not mistake the length of the hypotenuse for 2a, which is the sum of the two sides! Since the sum of the squares is 2a squared, the positive square root of 2 a is the exact length of the hypotenuse. Finally, ask a deeper question. If, instead of a square, we cut an equilateral triangle in half along the vertex perpendicular, how would we derive the ratio of the three sides of a right triangle with angles of 30 degrees, 60 degrees, and 90 degrees (1 to the root of 3 to 2)?
