5a × 2b =10 ab

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Steps 7–10

    Now, we list all the products (ab) or fractions (a over b) in parentheses, which are the key to the proof, n times, and then apply the idea of grouping n numbers of a together and n numbers of b together using the commutative and associative laws of multiplication. After this conversion, the structure of an expression that seemed complex can be clearly solved using basic operations that we already know.

  2. Steps 11–14

    Look at the = (5 times 2) times (a times b) combination rule shown on the screen. In the equation where (ab) is multiplied n times, if you collect only a, you get n numbers (a to the nth power), and if you collect only b, you get n numbers (b to the nth power). According to the principle that the exponent n outside the parentheses is distributed to each argument, the formula is derived without any gaps. What to pay special attention to at this stage is that when the power of a fraction (a over b) is raised to the nth power, the nth power must be applied not only to the numerator a but also to the denominator b to express it as the nth power of a over the nth power of b.

  3. Step 19

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting conditions given by the natural number exponents m and n and the definition of the power (the m square of a is the base a multiplied m times), the entire product (ab) or fraction (a over b) in the parentheses is listed n times, and then, through the commutative law of multiplication and the associative law, the idea of grouping n numbers of a together and n numbers of b together is obtained in the formula '5a times 2b = 10 ab'. We have clearly confirmed that the algebraic conclusion that the exponent law necessarily holds for all natural exponents holds true. The key point to remember here is that when the power of a fraction (a over b) is raised to the nth power, the nth power must be applied not only to the numerator a but also to the denominator b to express it as the nth power of a over the nth power of b.
