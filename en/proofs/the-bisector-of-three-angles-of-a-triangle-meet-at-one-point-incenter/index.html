The bisector of three angles of a triangle meet at one point(incenter) and the distance from this point(incenter) to the three sides is the same.

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Steps 8–12

    Center on the three green perpendicular lines (inscribed circle radii) drawn perpendicular to the three sides of the triangle at intersection I and the auxiliary line connecting the third vertex, and compare sides of the same length and angles of the same size one by one.

  2. Step 13

    This is the most important eureka moment in this proof! Look at the new green line segment drawn on the shape. It is an auxiliary line connecting the three green perpendicular lines (radius of the inscribed circle) drawn perpendicular to the three sides of the triangle at the intersection point I and the third vertex. A relationship that was not visible through the original shape alone is divided into two comparable shapes by this single green auxiliary line!

  3. Step 14

    Compare the two areas highlighted in orange and purple. The right triangles divided by the bisector of the two interior angles become RHA congruent with the common hypotenuse + right angle + acute angle congruent, making the distances to all three sides the same, and the two right triangles at the last vertex become RHS congruent, allowing you to see with your own eyes the principle of bisecting the third angle. Since the conditions for corresponding sides and angles are exactly met, the two shapes are perfectly congruent (or similar)!

  4. Step 15

    Look at the final mark highlighted in red on the shape! Since the size of the corresponding parts is the same due to the congruence and symmetry properties of the figure proven in the previous step, the bisectors of the three interior angles we aimed for meet at one point (incenter I), and the conclusion that the vertical distances from the incenter to all three sides are the same (radius r of the inscribed circle) has finally been proven!

  5. Steps 16–17

    The right triangles divided by the bisector of the two internal angles become RHA congruent with the common hypotenuse + right angle + acute angle congruent, making the distances to all three sides the same, and the two right triangles at the last vertex become RHS congruent, completing the principle of bisecting the third angle. What you must remember at this time is that the circumcenter has the same distance to the 'three vertices' (OA=OB=OC), and the incenter has the same distance to the 'feet of the perpendicular lines on the three sides' (ID=IE=IF=r). It is also directly connected to the formula for the area of the triangle: S = 1/2·r·(a+b+c).

  6. Steps 18–21

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence between shapes, we can confirm that the conclusion that the bisectors of the three interior angles we wanted to prove meet at one point (incenter I), and that the vertical distances from the incenter to all three sides are the same (radius r of the inscribed circle) is inevitably established.

  7. Step 22

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the assumption that the perpendicular lines are drawn on each of the three sides at the intersection point I of the bisectors of the two interior angles of the triangle, which was the starting condition, through the three green perpendicular lines (radius of the inscribed circle) drawn perpendicular to the three sides of the triangle at intersection I and the auxiliary line connecting the third vertex, the conclusion is that the bisectors of the three interior angles meet at one point (incenter I), and that the vertical distances from the incenter to the three sides are all the same (radius of the inscribed circle). It has been clearly confirmed that this is true. The key point to remember here is that the circumcenter has the same distance to the 'three vertices' (OA=OB=OC), and the incenter has the same distance to the 'foot of the perpendicular line on the three sides' (ID=IE=IF=r). It is also directly connected to the formula for the area of the triangle: S = 1/2·r·(a+b+c). Finally, ask a deeper question.
