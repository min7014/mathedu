The bisector of the apex angle of the isosceles triangle divides the base vertically.

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 2

    Check the target shape to the right of the top arrow. Given the starting assumption on the left, the intention is to show why the conclusion that the bisector of the vertex angle on the right side of the arrow bisects the base BC perpendicularly (BD = CD, angle = 90 degrees) is always true. Never mix assumptions and conclusions, and now let's build a logical bridge in the proof diagram below!

  2. Step 3

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions given in the problem on the diagram. Here, the assumption that the vertex angle bisector (or perpendicular to the base) is drawn in an isosceles triangle with two sides AB and AC of equal length is depicted with a blue symbol.

  3. Steps 4–5

    In the starting condition, an isosceles triangle with two sides AB and AC of equal length, the assumptions of drawing the bisector of the vertex angle (or the perpendicular to the base) are drawn in order on the proof figure, clearly establishing the positional relationship between each point and line segment.

  4. Steps 11–12

    The two sides have the same length (AB=AC), the auxiliary line AD is the common side, and the two divided vertex angles have the same size, so the two triangles on both sides are SAS congruent, the corresponding sides have the same length, and the principle of dividing a 180-degree straight angle in half to make a 90-degree perpendicular is completed. What you must remember at this time is that in a regular triangle, the bisector of the vertex angle and the perpendicular bisector of the base do not coincide, and only in the symmetry of an isosceles triangle, where both sides have the same length.

  5. Step 13

    Compare the two areas highlighted in orange and purple. Since the two sides have the same length (AB=AC), the auxiliary line AD is the common side, and the two divided vertex angles have the same size, the two triangles on both sides are SAS congruent, the corresponding sides have the same length, and you can directly see the principle of dividing a 180-degree straight angle in half to become 90 degrees perpendicular. Since the conditions for corresponding sides and angles are exactly met, the two shapes are perfectly congruent (or similar)!

  6. Step 16

    Look at the final mark highlighted in red on the shape! Since the sizes of the corresponding parts are the same due to the congruence and symmetry properties of the figure proven in the previous step, the conclusion that the bisector of the vertex angle we were aiming for bisects the base BC perpendicularly (BD = CD, angle = 90 degrees) has finally been proven!

  7. Step 17

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence between shapes, we can confirm that the conclusion that the bisector of the vertex angle we were trying to prove bisects the base BC perpendicularly (BD = CD, angle = 90 degrees) is inevitably established.

  8. Step 18

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the assumption that the bisector of the vertex angle (or the perpendicular to the base) is drawn in an isosceles triangle in which the two sides AB and AC are of equal length, which was the starting condition, the green angle bisector AD, which divides the vertex angle A in half, is passed through the first auxiliary line drawn down to the base BC, and the conclusion is established that the bisector of the vertex angle vertically bisects the base BC (BD = CD, angle = 90 degrees). Confirmed clearly. The key point to remember here is that in a regular triangle, the vertex angle bisector and the base perpendicular bisector do not coincide, and only in the symmetry of an isosceles triangle, where both sides have the same length.
