lim(θ → 0) (sin θ/θ)=1

24-step visual proof · Limits & Calculus · Differential Calculus

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Proof at a glance

Starting condition
In a unit circle of radius R = 1 with acute central angle θ (0 < θ < π/2 radians), compare inscribed △OAB, sector OAB, and tangent △OAT
What we prove
Area inequality sin θ < θ < tan θ and the fundamental trigonometric limit lim(θ→0) (sin θ / θ) = 1
Key idea
Bound circular sector OAB (area ½θ) between inner triangle △OAB (area ½ sin θ) and outer right triangle △OAT (area ½ tan θ)
Proof logic
From ½ sin θ < ½ θ < ½ tan θ, dividing by ½ sin θ and taking reciprocals gives cos θ < (sin θ)/θ < 1; as θ → 0, cos θ → 1, so the Squeeze Theorem forces (sin θ)/θ → 1
Common misconception
The angle θ MUST be measured in radians so that the sector area is ½r²θ = ½θ; in degrees, a scaling factor of π/180 appears

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 2

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions given in the problem on the diagram. Here, the geometric starting conditions for inscribed triangle OAB, sector OAB, and tangent right triangle OAT, sharing the central angle theta (but 0 < theta < pi/2, radians) on a unit circle with a radius of length 1, are plotted with blue symbols.

  2. Step 3

    The geometric starting conditions for the inscribed triangle OAB, sector OAB, and tangent right triangle OAT, which share the central angle theta (however, 0 < theta < pi/2, radian) in a unit circle with a radius of 1, which is the starting condition, are drawn in turn on the proof figure and clearly establish the positional relationship between each point and line segment.

  3. Step 4

    This is the most important eureka moment in this proof! Look at the new green line segment drawn on the shape. It is an auxiliary line that compares the inclusion relationship of the three shapes by simultaneously drawing the perpendicular line (height sin theta) drawn from point B (cos theta, sin theta) on the unit circle to the x-axis and the tangent line (height tan theta) of the unit circle drawn from point A (1, 0). A relationship that was not visible through the original shape alone is divided into two comparable shapes by this single green auxiliary line!

  4. Step 7

    Compare the two areas highlighted in orange and purple. The areas of the three shapes are related in size: triangle OAB area (1/2 sin theta) < sector OAB area (1/2 theta) < right triangle OAT area (1/2 tan theta). Multiply by 2 to get sin theta < theta < tan theta, take the reciprocal to make cos theta < (sin theta)/theta < 1, and then when it is 0 in theta, it becomes 1 in cos theta. Therefore, you can directly see the principle that the limit value of 1 is proven by the tightening theorem (sandwich theorem). Since the conditions for corresponding sides and angles are exactly met, the two shapes are perfectly congruent (or similar)!

  5. Step 14

    Check the target shape to the right of the top arrow. Given the starting assumption on the left, the sin theta < theta < tan theta area inequality is established at 0 < theta < pi/2 on the right side of the arrow. This means that if you divide both sides by sin theta and take the 0 limit from theta, the conclusion that (sin theta / theta) = 1 always holds true when the limit theta goes to 0. Never mix assumptions and conclusions, and now let's build a logical bridge in the proof diagram below!

  6. Steps 15–19

    The areas of the three shapes are related in size: triangle OAB area (1/2 sin theta) < sector OAB area (1/2 theta) < right triangle OAT area (1/2 tan theta). Multiply by 2 to get sin theta < theta < tan theta, take the reciprocal to make cos theta < (sin theta)/theta < 1, and then when it is 0 in theta, it becomes 1 in cos theta. Therefore, this is the stage where the principle of proving the limit value of 1 by the tightening theorem (sandwich theorem) is completed. What you must remember at this time is that when limit theta goes to 0 (sin theta / theta) = 1, if you use L'Hopital's theorem (denominator/numerator differentiation) in the proof, you will fall into circular logic! This is because the differential formula for the sine function itself is derived from the limit of this area inequality.

  7. Steps 20–22

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence relationship of the shapes, we can confirm that the area inequality of sin theta < theta < tan theta is established in 0 < theta < pi/2, which we wanted to prove. If we divide both sides by sin theta and take the 0 limit from theta, we can see that the conclusion that (sin theta / theta) = 1 is inevitably established when the limit theta goes to 0.

  8. Step 23

    Look at the final mark highlighted in red on the shape! Because the sizes of the corresponding parts are the same due to the congruence and symmetry properties of the shapes proven in the previous step, the sin theta < theta < tan theta area inequality is established in our goal of 0 < theta < pi/2. If we divide both sides by sin theta and take the 0 limit from theta, the conclusion that (sin theta / theta) = 1 is finally proven when the limit theta goes to 0!

  9. Step 24

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the geometric starting conditions in which the inscribed triangle OAB, sector OAB, and tangent right triangle OAT sharing the central angle theta (where 0 < theta < pi/2, radians) are drawn from a unit circle with a radius of 1, the perpendicular line (height sin theta) drawn from the point B (cos theta, sin theta) on the unit circle to the x-axis and the unit circle drawn from point A (1, 0). By constructing a tangent line (height tan theta) simultaneously and using an auxiliary line to compare the inclusion relationship of the three shapes, it was clearly confirmed that the area inequality holds for 0 < theta < pi/2 and sin theta < theta < tan theta. Dividing both sides by sin theta and taking the 0 limit at theta leads to the conclusion that (sin theta / theta) = 1 when the limit theta goes to 0. The key point to remember here is that when limit theta goes to 0 (sin theta / theta) = 1, if you use L'Hopital's theorem (denominator/numerator differentiation) in the proof, you will fall into circular logic! This is because the differential formula for the sine function itself is derived from the limit of this area inequality. Finally, ask a deeper question. Using this basic limit limit theta goes to 0 (sin theta / theta) = 1, why is the limit of [(1 - cos theta) / theta squared] exactly 1/2 when limit theta goes to 0?
