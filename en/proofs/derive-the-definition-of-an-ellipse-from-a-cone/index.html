Derive the definition of an ellipse from a cone

34-step visual proof · Conics & Vectors · Conic Sections

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Steps 1–4

    When an inclined plane slices completely across a right circular cone, it forms a closed oval curve. Let us prove why every point P on this section has a constant sum of distances to two fixed foci by nestling two tangent spheres inside the cone.

  2. Steps 5–11

    Inside the cone, place an upper sphere tangent to both the cone wall and the top of the cutting plane at point F1, and a lower sphere tangent to both the cone wall and the underside of the cutting plane at point F2.

  3. Steps 12–19

    Step 12—the key auxiliary line! Each sphere touches the cone along a horizontal tangent circle. Draw the cone generatrix through the apex and any point P on our curve; it intersects the upper and lower tangent circles at T1 and T2.

  4. Steps 20–27

    In 3D space, all tangent segments drawn from an external point P to the same sphere have identical length! Therefore PF1 equals PT1 on the upper sphere, and PF2 equals PT2 on the lower sphere.

  5. Steps 28–34

    Step 28—Eureka! Adding the two equalities gives PF1 + PF2 = PT1 + PT2. Since P lies right between T1 and T2 along the cone's straight generatrix, PT1 + PT2 equals the fixed cone segment length between the two parallel circles—proving the ellipse focal definition!
