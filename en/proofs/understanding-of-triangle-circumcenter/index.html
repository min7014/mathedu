Understanding of Triangle Circumcenter

15-step visual proof · Geometry · Euclidean Plane Geometry

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 2

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions given in the problem on the diagram. Here, the assumption that the perpendicular line is drawn from the intersection point O of the perpendicular bisectors of the two sides of the triangle to the remaining third side is depicted with a blue symbol.

  2. Step 3

    Check the target shape to the right of the top arrow. Given the starting assumption on the left, the perpendicular bisectors of the three sides to the right of the arrow must meet at one point (circumcenter O), and the distances from the circumcenter to the three vertices A, B, and C are all the same (OA = OB = OC, radius R of the circumscribed circle). This means that we will show why the conclusion is always true. Never mix assumptions and conclusions, and now let's build a logical bridge in the proof diagram below!

  3. Step 4

    From O, the intersection point of the perpendicular bisectors of the two sides of the triangle, which is the starting condition, draw the assumption that the perpendicular line is placed on the remaining third side in order on the proof figure, clearly establishing the positional relationship between each point and line segment.

  4. Steps 5–8

    Compare sides of the same length and angles of the same size in the shape one by one, centered on the three green radial auxiliary lines that directly connect the three vertices A, B, and C of the triangle at the intersection point O of the perpendicular bisector.

  5. Step 9

    Look at the final mark highlighted in red on the shape! Since the sizes of the corresponding parts are the same due to the congruence and symmetry properties of the figure proven in the previous step, the perpendicular bisectors of the three sides we aimed for must meet at one point (circumcenter O), and the distance from the circumcenter to the three vertices A, B, and C is all the same (OA = OB = OC, radius of the circumscribed circle R).

  6. Step 10

    This is the most important eureka moment in this proof! Look at the new green line segment drawn on the shape. These are the three green radial auxiliary lines that directly connect the three vertices A, B, and C of the triangle from the intersection point O of the perpendicular bisector. A relationship that was not visible through the original shape alone is divided into two comparable shapes by this single green auxiliary line!

  7. Step 11

    The right triangles divided by the perpendicular bisectors of the first and second sides become SAS congruent, establishing OA = OB = OC. Through this, the two right triangles on the perpendicular to the third side become RHS congruent, and the chain congruence principle is completed in which the third side is also exactly bisected. What you must remember at this time is that the triangle divided by the circumcenter O is not two opposite triangles, but 'two adjacent right triangles on one side' are congruent (isosceles triangle), and the circumcenter of an obtuse triangle is located outside the triangle.

  8. Steps 12–14

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence relationship of shapes, we can confirm that the conclusion that the perpendicular bisectors of the three sides we wanted to prove necessarily meet at one point (circumcenter O), and that the distances from the circumcenter to the three vertices A, B, and C are all the same (OA = OB = OC, radius R of the circumscribed circle) is inevitably established.

  9. Step 15

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting condition O, which was the intersection point of the perpendicular bisectors of the two sides of the triangle, the perpendicular line is drawn from the remaining third side, and through three green radial auxiliary lines that directly connect the three vertices A, B, and C of the triangle from the intersection O of the perpendicular bisectors. The perpendicular bisectors of the three sides must meet at one point (circumcenter O), and the distances from the circumcenter to the three vertices A, B, and C are all the same (OA = OB = OC, The radius of the circumcircle (R) clearly confirmed that the conclusion holds. The key point to remember here is that the triangle divided by the circumcenter O is not two opposite triangles but two adjacent right triangles on one side, which are congruent (isosceles triangles), and the circumcenter of an obtuse triangle is located outside the triangle. Finally, ask a deeper question. If the triangle is a right triangle, the circumcenter O will be located exactly in the middle of which side (the midpoint of the hypotenuse)?
