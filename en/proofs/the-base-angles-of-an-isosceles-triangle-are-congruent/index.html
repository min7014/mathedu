The base angles of an isosceles triangle are congruent.

14-step visual proof · Geometry · Euclidean Plane Geometry

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 2

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions given in the problem on the diagram. Here, the definition (assumption) of an isosceles triangle that the lengths of the two sides AB and AC in triangle ABC are equal (AB = AC) is depicted in blue symbols.

  2. Steps 8–9

    Since the two sides AB and AC are the same (assumed), the green line segment AD in the middle is the common side, and half of the vertex angle, which is the included angle, is the same, the two triangles ABD and ACD are SAS congruent, and the principle that the two base angles, which are corresponding angles, are the same in size is completed. What you must remember at this time is that in order to avoid using the conclusion to be proved (the two base angles are equal) in advance during the proof process, you must draw an auxiliary line bisector of the vertex angles and derive the SAS congruence to avoid falling into circular logic.

  3. Step 11

    Compare the two areas highlighted in orange and purple. Since the two sides AB and AC are the same (assumed), the green line segment AD in the middle is the common side, and half of the apex angle, which is the included angle, is equal to each other, the two triangles ABD and ACD are SAS congruent, and you can see with your own eyes the principle that the two base angles, which are corresponding angles, have the same size. Since the conditions for corresponding sides and angles are exactly met, the two shapes are perfectly congruent (or similar)!

  4. Step 14

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the definition (assumption) of an isosceles triangle that the lengths of the two sides AB and AC are the same in triangle ABC, which was the starting condition (AB = AC), and through the critical auxiliary line of drawing the green angle bisector AD, which divides the vertex angle A exactly in half, to the base BC, it was clearly confirmed that the conclusion was established that the two base angles B and C opposite the two sides of the same length have the same size (angle B = angle C). The key point to remember here is that in order to avoid using the conclusion to be proved (the two base angles are the same) in advance during the proof process, you must draw an auxiliary line bisector of the vertex angles and derive the SAS congruence to avoid falling into circular logic. Finally, ask a deeper question. If, instead of the bisector of the vertex angle, we draw an auxiliary line (median line) connecting the midpoint M of the base BC, under what congruence condition (SSS congruence) can we prove the same?
