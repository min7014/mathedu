Ceva's Theorem — Step-by-Step Visual Proof via Triangle Area Ratios

Proof at a glance

Starting condition
Three cevians AD, BE, and CF drawn from the vertices of △ABC intersect at a single interior point P
What we prove
The cyclic product of segment ratios around the perimeter satisfies (AF/FB) × (BD/DC) × (CE/EA) = 1
Key idea
Partition △ABC around concurrency point P into three sub-triangles △PAB, △PBC, and △PCA, comparing triangles that share a common cevian base
Proof logic
Because triangles with the same altitude have areas proportional to their bases, AF/FB = Area(△PCA)/Area(△PBC), BD/DC = Area(△PAB)/Area(△PCA), and CE/EA = Area(△PBC)/Area(△PAB), whose product cancels to 1
Common misconception
Always alternate vertex → division point → vertex in a single cyclic direction around the perimeter
What if not?
How does Ceva's Theorem immediately prove the concurrency of a triangle's medians (centroid), altitudes (orthocenter), and angle bisectors (incenter)?

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 1

    In this step-by-step visual proof, we will prove Ceva's Theorem, showing why three concurrent lines from the vertices of a triangle divide the opposite sides into ratios whose product is always 1.

  2. Step 2

    Starting on a clean board, we will construct our reference triangle and its interior intersection point step by step.

  3. Step 4

    We extend the three sides of triangle A B C with dashed lines to clearly visualize the lines containing each side.

  4. Step 6

    Drawing straight lines from vertices A, B, and C through point O, we label their intersections with the opposite sides BC, CA, and AB as points D, E, and F respectively.

  5. Step 7

    Now let us trace around the perimeter. Starting at vertex A and moving to point F on side AB, we highlight segment AF with a red arc and place AF in the numerator of our first ratio.

  6. Step 8

    Continuing from point F to vertex B, we highlight segment FB in purple and place it in the denominator, forming the first ratio AF over FB.

  7. Step 9

    Next, from vertex B to point D on side BC, we mark segment BD with a blue arc as the numerator of our second ratio.

  8. Step 10

    From point D to vertex C, we mark segment DC in the denominator, multiplying by the second ratio BD over DC.

  9. Step 11

    Continuing around the triangle from vertex C to point E on side CA, we highlight segment CE in green as the third numerator.

  10. Step 12

    Finally, returning from point E back to our starting vertex A, we highlight segment EA in orange to complete the third ratio CE over EA.

  11. Step 13

    Ceva's Theorem states that as we alternate vertex-to-point-to-vertex all the way around the triangle, the product AF over FB, times BD over DC, times CE over EA, is always equal to 1.

  12. Step 14

    Why does this product always equal 1? Looking at line CO as a shared base line, the segment ratio AF over FB equals the area of triangle AOC divided by the area of triangle COB.

  13. Step 15

    By the exact same altitude principle along line AO, the ratio BD over DC equals the area of triangle BOA divided by the area of triangle AOC.

  14. Step 16

    Likewise, along line BO, the ratio CE over EA equals the area of triangle COB divided by the area of triangle BOA. Multiplying all three area fractions together, every triangle area cancels in numerator and denominator, leaving exactly 1!

  15. Step 17

    Visit min7014.github.io to drag the intersection point O interactively and verify that Ceva's Theorem holds for every configuration!

Formal proof verification (Lean 4)

✔ Machine-checked in Lean 4 (0 sorry)

Given positive sub-triangle areas sA, sB, sC > 0 and shared-altitude area proportions along sides BC, CA, AB, cancel positive area factors in Lean 4 to prove bd * ce * af = dc * ea * fb.

View Lean 4 theorem source (ceva_area_ratio_concurrency)
-- 2. [2014111902] Ceva's Theorem via Triangle Area Ratios
theorem ceva_area_ratio_concurrency
    (bd dc ce ea af fb sA sB sC : Int)
    (hsA : sA > 0) (hsB : sB > 0) (hsC : sC > 0)
    (h1 : bd * sC = dc * sB)
    (h2 : ce * sA = ea * sC)
    (h3 : af * sB = fb * sA) :
    bd * ce * af = dc * ea * fb := by
  have hprod : (bd * ce * af) * (sA * sB * sC) = (dc * ea * fb) * (sA * sB * sC) := by
    calc
      (bd * ce * af) * (sA * sB * sC)
          = (bd * sC) * (ce * sA) * (af * sB) := by grind
      _   = (dc * sB) * (ea * sC) * (fb * sA) := by rw [h1, h2, h3]
      _   = (dc * ea * fb) * (sA * sB * sC) := by grind
  have hs : sA * sB * sC ≠ 0 := by
    have h12 : 0 < sA * sB := Int.mul_pos hsA hsB
    have h123 : 0 < sA * sB * sC := Int.mul_pos h12 hsC
    omega
  exact Int.eq_of_mul_eq_mul_right hs hprod
