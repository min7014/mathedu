(a^m)^n=a^(mn)( m, n are natural numbers.)

13-step visual proof · Exponentials, Logs, Trig & Sequences · Exponentials & Logarithms

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Steps 5–7

    Look at the = (a times · · · times a) relationship shown on the screen. Now, we apply the idea of algebraic decomposition, which is the key to the proof, by expanding the equation in which all the parentheses (a to the m power) are multiplied n times and counting the structure in which a total of n rows of m bundles of a are arranged. After this conversion, the structure of an expression that seemed complex can be clearly solved using basic operations that we already know.

  2. Steps 8–9

    Look at the relationship shown on the screen: = a to m squared times n = a to mn squared. If you add m bundles of a n times, m plus m plus... (n times) = m times n, the equation is derived without any gaps according to the principle of multiplication of exponents (a to the mn power). One thing to be especially careful about in this step is, don't confuse (a to the mth power) to the nth power and a to the mth power times a to the nthth power! For powers outside parentheses, the exponents are multiplied (mn), and for powers with the same base, the exponents are added together (m+n).

  3. Step 13

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting conditions given the natural number exponents m and n and the definition of the power (the m squared of a is the base a multiplied m times), the entire parenthesis (m squared of a) is multiplied n times, and through the idea of algebraic decomposition to count the structure in which a total of n rows of m bundles of a are arranged, the formula 'm, n are natural numbers, the n squared of (a m squared) = a. It was clearly confirmed that the algebraic conclusion that the exponent law of 'mn squared' necessarily holds for all natural exponents holds true. The key point to remember here is, don't confuse (a to the m squared) to the nth power and a to the m squared times a to the nth power! For powers outside parentheses, the exponents are multiplied (mn), and for powers with the same base, the exponents are added together (m+n). Finally, ask a deeper question. If the exponents m and n extend beyond natural numbers to 0 or negative integers (a to the power of 0 = 1, a to the minus nth power = 1 over the nth power of a), or even to rational exponents, what additional conditions (base a > 0) are needed?
