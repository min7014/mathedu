Pythagoras Tree

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 1

    In this step-by-step visual proof lecture, we explore the Pythagoras Tree, where right triangles and squares branch out infinitely while preserving total area.

  2. Step 2

    Starting from a clean canvas, let us construct the base square and the generating right triangle one step at a time.

  3. Step 3

    First, we draw the trunk square at the bottom, and place a right triangle on its top edge using a semicircle. By Thales' theorem, any angle inscribed in a semicircle is always a ninety-degree right angle.

  4. Step 4

    Next, we construct two smaller squares on the two legs of the right triangle. By the Pythagorean Theorem, the sum of the areas of these two new squares is exactly equal to the area of the trunk square below.

  5. Step 5

    On top of those two squares, we attach similar right triangles and sprout four new branch squares. Remarkably, the combined area of all four squares in this third level still equals the single trunk square.

  6. Step 6

    In the fourth generation, eight squares branch outward. While the number of squares doubles at every level as powers of two, the total area at each level remains strictly invariant.

  7. Step 7

    Now sixteen squares form the fifth level of branches. Notice on the right how the acute angle of the generating right triangle controls the tilt and curvature of the entire tree.

  8. Step 8

    Thirty-two squares bloom across the sixth level. Zooming into any single branch reveals an exact miniature copy of the whole structure, demonstrating the self-similarity of fractal geometry.

  9. Step 9

    Sixty-four squares now create a lush canopy. Through the simple recursive repetition of the Pythagorean Theorem, pure geometry reproduces the organic branching patterns found in nature.

  10. Step 10

    Here is the fully grown Pythagoras Tree at the eighth stage. Take a moment to appreciate how classical area conservation and modern fractal geometry unite in visual harmony.

  11. Step 11

    Visit the interactive geometry lab at min7014.github.io to drag the vertex along the semicircle yourself and watch the Pythagoras Tree transform dynamically in real time!
