If a triangle has 2 conguent angles, then it is isosceles.

14-step visual proof · Geometry · Euclidean Plane Geometry

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 3

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions given in the problem on the diagram. Here, the assumption that the two interior angles B and C at both ends of the base in triangle ABC are the same size is depicted in blue symbols.

  2. Step 4

    In the starting condition, triangle ABC, the assumption that the two interior angles B and C at both ends of the base are the same size is drawn in order on the proof figure, clearly establishing the positional relationship between each point and line segment.

  3. Steps 8–9

    The sum of the three interior angles of the triangle is 180 degrees, so the remaining angles are equal, and since the common side AD and both end angles are equal, ASA congruence is established, which completes the principle that the lengths of the corresponding sides AB and AC are equal. What you must remember at this time is that the properties of an isosceles triangle (if two sides are equal, the two base angles are equal) and the conditions for becoming an isosceles triangle (if the two angles are equal, the two sides are equal) are inverse propositions in which the assumption and conclusion are interchanged.

  4. Step 11

    Compare the two areas highlighted in orange and purple. The sum of the three interior angles of a triangle is 180 degrees, so the remaining angles are equal, and since the common side AD and both end angles are equal, ASA congruence is established, so you can directly see the principle that the lengths of the corresponding sides AB and AC are equal. Since the conditions for corresponding sides and angles are exactly met, the two shapes are perfectly congruent (or similar)!

  5. Step 12

    Look at the final mark highlighted in red on the shape! Because the sizes of the corresponding parts become the same due to the congruence and symmetry properties of the shapes proven in the previous step, the lengths of the two opposing sides AB and AC that we aimed for also become the same, making it an isosceles triangle. It has finally been proven!

  6. Step 14

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the assumption that the two interior angles B and C at both ends of the base in triangle ABC, which was the starting condition, are the same size, we clearly confirmed that through the green auxiliary line drawn by drawing the bisector of the vertex angle A down to the base BC, the lengths of the two opposite sides AB and AC are also the same, forming an isosceles triangle. The key point to remember here is that the properties of an isosceles triangle (if two sides are equal, then the base angles are equal) and the conditions for becoming an isosceles triangle (if the two angles are equal, then both sides are equal) are inverse propositions in which the assumption and conclusion are interchanged. Finally, ask a deeper question. If all three interior angles are the same size (60 degrees), what kind of triangle will it be?
