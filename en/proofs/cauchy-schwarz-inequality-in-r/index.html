  5. Steps 24–32

    Since the inscribed angle to the diameter is 90 degrees, the similarity ratio makes the perpendicular height PH = root ab, and in a right triangle OPH, the hypotenuse (radius (a+b)/2) is always greater than or equal to the right angle side (height root ab). This is the stage in which the principle of absolute inequality is completed at a glance through geometric intuition. What you must remember at this time is that when finding the minimum value using an arithmetic/geometric mean inequality, you must check whether both numbers are 'positive' and verify whether there is a real number x that satisfies the equality condition (when the two terms are equal to each other)!

  6. Steps 33–39

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence relationship between shapes, we can confirm that for any two positive numbers a and b that we wanted to prove, the inequality of arithmetic mean (a+b)/2 or more, geometric mean root ab or more, harmonic mean 2ab/(a+b) is always established, and the conclusion that the equality sign holds only when a = b is inevitably established.

  7. Step 40

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the geometric starting conditions, draw a semicircle whose diameter is the length (a+b) of the two positive numbers a and b, which were the starting conditions, and draw a perpendicular line to the semicircle at the boundary point of the two line segments. Create a right triangle with the radius OP (arithmetic mean (a+b)/2) connecting the center O of the semicircle and the intersection point P on the semicircle, the height of the perpendicular line PH (geometric mean root ab), and the perpendicular line drawn from H to OP (harmonic mean piece).
