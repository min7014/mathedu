RHA Congruent : If two right-angled triangles have their hypotenuses equal in length, and a pair of acute angles are equal in measurement, then the triangles are congruent.

14-step visual proof · Geometry · Euclidean Plane Geometry

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 2

    In the starting condition of two right triangles (R, 90 degrees), the hypotenuse length (H) and the size of one acute angle (A) are the same. Draw the assumption that the length of the hypotenuse (H) and the size of one acute angle (A) are the same. Draw one by one on the proof figure and clearly establish the positional relationship between each point and line segment.

  2. Step 5

    Geometric manipulation that joins two right triangles into one large triangle by joining adjacent right angle sides of the same length. Compare sides of the same length and angles of the same size in a shape one by one, focusing on the auxiliary line.

  3. Steps 8–10

    Since two right angles of 90 degrees meet and form a straight angle of 180 degrees, the large triangle becomes an isosceles triangle with two sides (hypotenuse H) of equal length. Due to the properties of isosceles triangles, the bisector of the vertex angle bisects the base, thus completing the principle of ASA congruence. What you must remember at this time is that in a regular triangle, two sides are not congruent even if the non-included angle (SSA) is the same, but in a right triangle (90 degrees), RHS congruence is always established thanks to the symmetry of isosceles triangles!

  4. Step 14

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the assumption that the length of the hypotenuse (H) and the size of an acute angle (A) are the same in two right triangles (R, 90 degrees), which were the starting conditions, we went through the geometric manipulation auxiliary line to join the two right triangles into one large triangle by joining right angle neighboring sides of the same length, and it was clearly confirmed that the conclusion that the two right triangles are completely overlapped is RHA congruence. The key point to remember here is that in a regular triangle, two sides are not congruent even if the non-included angle (SSA) is the same, but in a right triangle (90 degrees), RHS congruence is always established thanks to the symmetry of isosceles triangles! Finally, ask a deeper question. In a regular acute triangle that does not require a right angle (90 degrees), why can there be two shapes if only two sides and one angle, not the included angle, are given?
