Menelaus' Theorem — Step-by-Step Visual Proof with Parallel Auxiliary Line

20-step visual proof · Geometry · Euclidean Plane Geometry

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Proof at a glance

Starting condition
A transversal line intersects the sides (or extended sides) AB, BC, and CA of △ABC at points F, D, and E respectively
What we prove
The cyclic product of directed segment ratios around the triangle satisfies (AF/FB) × (BD/DC) × (CE/EA) = 1
Key idea
Draw an auxiliary line through vertex C parallel to side AB (or parallel to the transversal line), meeting the transversal at point K
Proof logic
Using AA similarity of the two pairs of triangles formed by the parallel auxiliary line, express the ratios in terms of segment CK so that every intermediate term telescopes and cancels to 1
Common misconception
Do not confuse Menelaus' Theorem (collinear points on a transversal line, proven via a parallel auxiliary line) with Ceva's Theorem (concurrent lines meeting at a point P, proven via triangle area ratios)
What if not?
How does the converse of Menelaus' Theorem prove that three points in a geometric configuration are collinear?

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 1

    In this step-by-step visual lecture, we will prove Menelaus' Theorem, showing how any transversal line cutting across a triangle creates three directed segment ratios whose product equals 1.

  2. Step 4

    We extend all three sides of triangle A B C with dashed lines so that external intersections can be seen clearly.

  3. Step 5

    Now we draw a purple transversal line that cuts across two sides of the triangle and the extension of the third side.

  4. Step 6

    We label the intersection points: point R on side AB, point Q on side AC, and point P on the extension of side BC.

  5. Step 7

    Let us trace the loop around the triangle. From vertex A to point R, we mark segment AR with a red arc as the first numerator.

  6. Step 8

    From point R to vertex B, we mark segment RB in purple, completing the first ratio AR over RB.

  7. Step 9

    Next, from vertex B all the way out to the external point P, we mark segment BP with a blue arc as the second numerator.

  8. Step 10

    Returning from external point P to vertex C, we mark segment PC in the denominator, forming the second ratio BP over PC.

  9. Step 11

    Continuing from vertex C to point Q on side AC, we highlight segment CQ in green as the third numerator.

  10. Step 12

    Finally, from point Q back to the starting vertex A, we highlight segment QA in orange to complete the ratio CQ over QA.

  11. Step 13

    Menelaus' Theorem states that around the cycle A to R to B to P to C to Q to A, the product AR over RB, times BP over PC, times CQ over QA, is always equal to 1.

  12. Step 14

    How do we prove this? Here is the key auxiliary line: through vertex C, draw a line parallel to the transversal RP, intersecting side AB at point D!

  13. Step 15

    In triangle BPR, because line DC is parallel to line RP, parallel line similarity tells us that BP over PC equals RB over DR.

  14. Step 16

    Rearranging this first similarity equation shows that BP over PC times DR over RB equals 1.

  15. Step 17

    Next, look at triangle ARQ. Since segment DC is parallel to segment RQ, similarity again gives CQ over QA equals RD over AR.

  16. Step 18

    Rearranging this second relation shows that CQ over QA times AR over RD also equals 1.

  17. Step 19

    Substituting RB over DR for BP over PC, and RD over AR for CQ over QA, every segment AR, RB, and RD cancels out in numerator and denominator, proving the product is 1!

  18. Step 20

    Visit min7014.github.io to move the transversal line interactively and see Menelaus' Theorem in action!
