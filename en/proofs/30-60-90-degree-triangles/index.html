30-60-90 Degree Triangles

32-step visual proof · Geometry · Euclidean Plane Geometry

Original step-by-step proof (PDF) Construct it in MathLab Split-screen study board Source page & applets

Proof at a glance

Starting condition
A 30°-60°-90° right triangle formed by dropping an altitude from one vertex of an equilateral triangle of side length 2a
What we prove
Exact side ratio opposite 30°, 60°, and 90° is 1 : √3 : 2 (so sin 30° = 1/2, cos 30° = √3/2, tan 30° = 1/√3)
Key idea
Reflect the 30°-60°-90° right triangle across its altitude (or bisect an equilateral triangle of side 2a with its perpendicular altitude AH)
Proof logic
The altitude bisects the base of length 2a into BH = a; applying the Pythagorean Theorem gives height AH = √((2a)² - a²) = √3·a, proving 1 : √3 : 2 without rote memorization
Common misconception
Remember that 2 is the hypotenuse (longest side), while √3 ≈ 1.732 is the leg opposite the 60° angle
What if not?
How do these exact ratios extend to all four quadrants on the unit circle in trigonometry?

  3. Step 7

    This is the most important eureka moment in this proof! Look at the new green line segment drawn on the shape. It is an auxiliary perpendicular line that bisects two congruent right triangles by lowering the foot of the perpendicular line H from the vertex A of the equilateral triangle ABC, whose side length is 2a, to the base BC (or restoring an equilateral triangle by joining two 30-60-90 triangles). A relationship that was not visible through the original shape alone is divided into two comparable shapes by this single green auxiliary line!

  4. Steps 11–18

    Place the foot H of the perpendicular line from the vertex A of the equilateral triangle ABC, whose side length is 2a, to the base BC, and compare the sides of the same length and angles of the same size one by one in the figure centered on the auxiliary perpendicular line that bisects the two congruent right triangles (or restores an equilateral triangle by joining two 30-60-90 triangles).

  5. Step 19

    The bisector of the vertex angle (60 degrees) of an equilateral triangle perpendicularly bisects the base (2a), so the base piece BH = a, and by the Pythagorean theorem (square of (2a) - square of a = square of 3a), height AH = root 3 a, which completes the principle of proving 1 to root 3 to 2. The important thing to remember here is that in 1 to 2 to root 3, the longest side is not the root 3 (about 1.732), but the hypotenuse, 2! 2 must correspond to the hypotenuse opposite 90 degrees.

  6. Step 20

    Look at the final mark highlighted in red on the shape! Since the size of the corresponding parts becomes the same due to the congruence and symmetry properties of the shapes proven in the previous step, the ratio of the lengths of the 30-degree opposite (base) to the 60-degree opposite (height) to the 90-degree opposite (hypotenuse) in the 30-degree, 60-degree, and 90-degree right triangles we were aiming for is finally proven to be always 1 to the root of 3 to 2!

  7. Step 32

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting conditions given by a special right triangle with three interior angles of 30 degrees, 60 degrees, and 90 degrees (a right triangle in which an equilateral triangle with a side length of 2a is bisected by the vertex perpendicular), an auxiliary perpendicular line bisects the two congruent right triangles by lowering the foot of the perpendicular line H from the vertex A of the equilateral triangle ABC with a side length of 2a to the base BC (or After joining two 30-60-90 triangles to restore an equilateral triangle, it was clearly confirmed that in 30-degree, 60-degree, and 90-degree right triangles, the ratio of the lengths of the 30-degree opposite side (base) to the 60-degree opposite side (height) to the 90-degree opposite side (hypotenuse) is always 1 to the root of 3 to 2. The key point to remember here is that in 1 to 2 to root 3, the longest side is not the root 3 (about 1.732), but the hypotenuse, 2! 2 must correspond to the hypotenuse opposite 90 degrees.
