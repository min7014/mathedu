Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Step 2

    Look at the upper left area of the screen. When starting a proof, first clearly indicate the assumptions, which are the starting conditions given in the problem, on the diagram. Here, the starting conditions for the 'sphere base curve', which connects points and line segments with certain geometric rules to the edges and tangent planes of a sphere or tetrahedron in three-dimensional space, are drawn with blue symbols.

  2. Steps 3–4

    Sides of the same length and angles of the same size in a shape are paired and compared one by one, centering on the parametric line segment (string) that connects the equal points on the edge in one-to-one correspondence and the perpendicular line drawn from the reference point to the tangent plane.

  3. Step 5

    Compare the two areas highlighted in orange and purple. When a straight line moves through an internal branch point at a constant rate, you can directly see the spatial geometric principle that the traces of the intersection of two neighboring straight lines form a quadratic curve and a three-dimensional scaffolding surface. Since the conditions for corresponding sides and angles are exactly met, the two shapes are perfectly congruent (or similar)!

  4. Steps 6–7

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence between shapes, we can confirm that the spatial geometry conclusion that smooth envelopes and three-dimensional curved surfaces are created when the collection of straight lines (normals and line segments) that we were trying to prove continuously rotates and intersects in space is inevitably established.

  5. Step 8

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the starting condition of the 'sphere curve', which connects points and line segments with certain geometric rules to the corners and tangent planes of a sphere or tetrahedron in a three-dimensional space, which was the starting condition, goes through the parametric line segments (strings) that connect equal points on the edges in one-to-one correspondence in order and the foot drawing of a perpendicular line from the reference point to the tangent plane, and then a smooth envelope line and a smooth envelope when a collection of straight lines (bundle of normal lines and line segments) continuously rotates and intersects in space. It was clearly confirmed that the spatial geometry conclusion that a three-dimensional curved surface emerges holds true. The key point to remember here is that it is easy to think that you need a curved tool to draw a curve or curved surface, but a perfect curved surface can be created with just a bunch of tangents of straight lines.
