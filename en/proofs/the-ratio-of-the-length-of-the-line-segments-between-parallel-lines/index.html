The Ratio of the Length of the Line Segments between Parallel Lines

Step-by-step walkthrough

  1. Steps 2–16

    The geometric starting conditions of 'ratio of lengths of line segments between parallel lines' given the starting conditions of parallel lines, midpoint, center of similarity or ruler, and compass drawing rules are drawn in order on the proof figure, clearly establishing the positional relationship between each point and line segment.

  2. Steps 17–30

    Draw one auxiliary line parallel to a given straight line to create a parallelogram (opposite sides are the same length) and an AA/SAS similar triangle at the same time. Centering on the first auxiliary line of Polya, compare sides of the same length and angles of the same size one by one in the shape.

  3. Steps 31–42

    This is the stage in which the principle of proving, step by step, that the ratio of the lengths of corresponding sides is constant is constant by deriving AA similarity (or SAS congruence/similarity) through coincidence of equal and opposite angles made by parallel lines is completed. What you must remember at this time is that when applying the side ratio of a parallel line and a triangle, you must indicate whether it is the length of the entire side or a partial piece so as not to confuse 'ratio of the side sides of a small triangle (a : a+b) = ratio of the base' and 'ratio of both pieces (a : b = a' : b')'!

  4. Steps 43–51

    Now let's combine the geometric clues we checked earlier into one. From the correspondence relationship between shapes, we can confirm that the conclusion that the proportional expression and geometric properties of 'ratio of the lengths of line segments between parallel lines' are always established through the properties of equal angles and opposite angles of parallel lines and the congruence and similarity of triangles that we tried to prove is inevitably established.

  5. Step 52

    The visual proof is complete! Starting from the geometric starting condition of 'the ratio of the lengths of line segments between parallel lines' given the starting condition of the center of parallel lines, midpoint, and similarity, or the rule of ruler and compass drawing, one auxiliary line parallel to the given straight line is drawn to create a parallelogram (the length of opposite sides is the same) and an AA/SAS similar triangle at the same time. Through Polya's first auxiliary line, 'the distance between parallel lines' is determined by the properties of equal and opposite angles of parallel lines and the congruence and similarity of triangles. It was clearly confirmed that the conclusion that the proportionality and geometric properties of the ratio of the lengths of line segments always holds true. The key point to remember here is that when applying the side ratio of a parallel line and a triangle, you must indicate whether it is the length of the entire side or a partial piece so as not to confuse the 'ratio of the side sides of a small triangle (a : a+b) = ratio of the base' and 'the ratio of both pieces (a : b = a' : b')'!
